Selecting Materials for Your Toronto Bathroom Renovation

A bathroom renovation lives or dies on its materials — both how it looks and how much it costs. The same layout can read as builder-basic or genuinely luxurious depending on the tile, vanity and countertop you choose, and the wrong picks can fail building code or turn slippery the first wet morning. Here's how to choose tile, vanities, countertops and shower fixtures that look great, last, and pass inspection in Toronto.

Work through the bathroom surface by surface. Each material has a clear best-in-class option for our climate and code — once you know it, selection gets a lot simpler.

Key Takeaways

  • Floor tile should be porcelain with a slip rating (COF) of 0.42 or higher.
  • Subway or large-format porcelain wall tile is timeless and resale-friendly.
  • Floating, solid-wood vanities beat MDF on durability and feel.
  • Quartz is the go-to countertop — non-porous and stain-resistant.
  • Ontario code requires at least a pressure-balanced shower valve.

Floor Tile

Bathroom floor tile needs to be slip-resistant (a COF rating of 0.42 or higher for wet areas), durable and easy to clean. Porcelain is the dominant choice — dense, water-resistant and available in every aesthetic from wood-look to marble-look. Natural stone such as slate or travertine is beautiful but requires sealing and more maintenance, and you should avoid highly polished stone on floors, where it becomes dangerously slick.

0.42 The minimum coefficient-of-friction (COF) slip rating you want for bathroom floor tile in wet areas.

Wall Tile

Subway tile in white or soft neutral tones remains a classic that photographs well and helps sell homes. Large-format porcelain slab — matching the floor or complementary — creates a seamless, luxurious look with fewer grout lines. Decorative accent tiles or a feature wall add visual interest without overwhelming a small space.

Vanities

Floating vanities are the modern standard — they make small bathrooms feel larger and make floor cleaning easier. Solid wood vanities with dovetail drawers are far more durable than MDF or particleboard. For main bathrooms, storage capacity matters most; for ensuites, aesthetics often take priority. Your bathroom renovation team can match the vanity to your storage needs and style.

Countertops

Quartz is the dominant bathroom countertop material in Toronto — non-porous, stain-resistant and available in hundreds of colours and patterns, including excellent marble looks. Natural marble is gorgeous but needs sealing and stains easily. Engineered stone gives you the best durability-to-aesthetics ratio for the money.

Shower Fixtures

Thermostatic shower systems, with separate volume and temperature controls, add comfort and are increasingly standard in mid-range renovations. A rain shower head combined with a handheld gives you a versatile setup. At minimum, choose a pressure-balanced valve — temperature-scald protection is a building code requirement in Ontario.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best floor tile for a bathroom?

Porcelain with a slip-resistance (COF) rating of 0.42 or higher is the dominant choice — dense, water-resistant and available in wood-look and marble-look finishes.

Is quartz or marble better for a bathroom countertop?

Quartz is better for most bathrooms — non-porous and stain-resistant with hundreds of patterns including marble looks. Natural marble is beautiful but needs sealing and stains easily.

Are floating vanities a good idea?

Yes. Floating vanities make small bathrooms feel larger and make floor cleaning easier. Choose solid wood with dovetail drawers over MDF for durability.

What shower valve does Ontario code require?

Ontario building code requires at least a pressure-balanced (temperature-protection) valve. Thermostatic systems add comfort with separate volume and temperature controls.

What wall tile sells homes best?

Subway tile in white or soft neutrals is a timeless choice that photographs well and appeals to buyers; large-format porcelain creates a seamless, premium look.

Should I use natural stone in a bathroom?

Natural stone like slate or travertine is beautiful but needs sealing and more maintenance. Avoid highly polished stone on floors, where it becomes slippery.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a bathroom renovation cost in Toronto?

Bathroom renovations in Toronto range from $8,000–$15,000 for a cosmetic refresh to $20,000–$45,000 for a full gut-and-rebuild. High-end projects with custom tile, steam showers, and heated floors can exceed $60,000. We provide detailed written quotes broken down by scope.

How long does a bathroom renovation take in Toronto?

A cosmetic bathroom update in Toronto takes 1–2 weeks. A full renovation with plumbing and tile work takes 3–5 weeks. Projects involving moving walls or relocating plumbing can take 6–8 weeks. We provide a clear timeline before work begins.

Do I need a permit for my bathroom renovation in Toronto?

In Toronto, permits are required when you're moving plumbing, adding electrical circuits, or removing walls. Replacing fixtures in the same location, new tile, or a vanity swap typically don't require permits. We pull all required permits and coordinate inspections.

Can you move the toilet or shower to a different location?

Yes, but relocating plumbing is the biggest cost driver in bathroom renovations. Moving a toilet or shower requires breaking the concrete floor (in basements) or rerouting drain lines — adding $3,000–$10,000 to the project. We advise on cost-effective alternatives where possible.

What tile is best for bathroom floors and shower walls?

Porcelain tile is the most durable and water-resistant choice for both floors and shower walls in Toronto homes. Large-format tiles (24×24") make small bathrooms look bigger. Natural stone is beautiful but requires sealing and more maintenance.

Do you handle electrical and plumbing in bathroom renovations?

Yes. We coordinate all trades including licensed electricians and plumbers as part of our full-service renovation approach in Toronto. You deal with one contractor — we handle the entire scope and scheduling.

What is the best ventilation solution for a bathroom?

Ontario Building Code requires mechanical ventilation in all bathrooms without operable windows. We install Energy Star–rated exhaust fans sized to the room's CFM requirements. Proper ventilation prevents mould, protects drywall, and extends the life of your finishes.

Can you add a bathroom to my basement?

Yes. Basement bathroom additions in Toronto typically require breaking the concrete floor to tie into the drain system (or installing a sewage ejector pump). Costs range from $8,000–$20,000 depending on complexity. We handle permits, rough-in plumbing, and all finishes.

What's included in a full bathroom renovation?

Our full bathroom renovations in Toronto include: demolition, waterproofing, plumbing rough-in, electrical, tile installation, vanity and fixture installation, glass shower enclosures, accessories, painting, and final inspection. We handle everything from start to finish.

How do I choose between a walk-in shower and a bathtub?

If you have only one bathroom, keep the tub — it helps with resale value and is needed for bathing children. If you have a second bathroom, converting the tub to a walk-in shower is a popular upgrade in Toronto that adds usable space and a modern look.
