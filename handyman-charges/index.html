How Much Do Handymen Charge in Toronto?

The total for a larger handyman project depends on the work included and the scope agreed in advance. For larger or combined jobs, we can discuss a negotiated project price after reviewing the details. Materials are separate, and any additional work must be priced and approved before it begins.

Key Takeaways

  • A project price can be discussed for larger or combined work after the scope is reviewed and agreed.
  • The agreed price should identify the work included before the project starts.
  • Materials and consumable supplies are paid for by the customer and are separate from labour.
  • Added work must be priced and approved before it begins.
  • For work billed by time, see our separate handyman hourly-rate guide.

What a Handyman Project Price Covers

A project price is based on the specific tasks and conditions reviewed with you. Before work starts, confirm which tasks are included, what is excluded, whether materials are separate, and how a request to add work will be handled. There is no single fixed price for every project.

Hourly or Project Pricing

Some visits are billed using the applicable hourly schedule. A negotiated project price may be discussed for a larger or combined scope once the work has been reviewed and agreed. See our hourly-rate guide for the rates and minimums; this page explains project pricing rather than listing a price for every task.

Materials and Consumable Supplies

The customer pays for materials and consumable supplies. If we purchase them for the job, their cost is charged separately from labour. For example, when we complete painting work, the customer pays for the paint and other job materials; our painting service page explains the service.

Agreeing on the Project Scope

For a larger or combined job, share the task list and available details so we can review the scope. We can then discuss a negotiated price for the agreed work. This may apply to general handyman, plumbing, or electrical work, subject to the project details and service availability. A discussed price is not a discount promise; it is a project price based on the confirmed scope.

Changes and Next Steps

The agreed price covers the work confirmed before the project starts. If you request additional work, it must be priced and approved before it begins. To request a review, use our handyman services hub or send us your project details.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I get a project price for a larger handyman job?

For larger or combined work, a negotiated project price may be discussed after the scope is reviewed. The included work and price must be agreed before work begins; added work must also be priced and approved in advance.

Do you charge hourly or by the project?

Are materials included in a project price?

The customer pays for materials and consumable supplies. If aMaximum purchases them for the job, their cost is charged separately from labour. Confirm material selection and costs before work begins.

What if I add work after agreeing on the scope?

Additional work must be priced and approved before it begins. The agreed project price covers only the scope confirmed with the customer.

Can plumbing and electrical work be included in a project price?

A combined scope can be reviewed, but plumbing and electrical tasks must be identified separately with their responsible qualified providers and applicable requirements. For paid residential electrical work in Ontario, confirm the Licensed Electrical Contractor. A project price does not waive those requirements.

Send the address, complete task list, photos, and the result you want through the handyman booking form. Include access restrictions and known material needs. We will review whether a project price is appropriate and confirm the included work, exclusions, and separately charged materials.

Frequently Asked Questions

What makes a task list suitable for project pricing?

A clearly described scope helps us review whether a project price is appropriate. List the tasks, desired result, access conditions, and known material needs. Suitability is reviewed individually; there is no automatic size or price threshold.

Where do hourly charges fit into a project-pricing discussion?

Some work is billed using the applicable hourly schedule. For larger or combined work, a negotiated project price may be discussed after the scope is reviewed and agreed. See the hourly-rate guide for the confirmed minimums and rates.

Does one project price mean one visit?

No. Price and scheduling are separate terms. Preparation, material delivery, drying, and coordination may require several visits even when the work is covered by one agreed project scope.

How should I describe the result I expect?

Describe the completed task and finish, not only the problem. For a wall repair, specify whether preparation, priming, and painting are requested. For assembly, identify the item, final location, and any anchoring work.

What should the written project scope exclude?

Identify work not covered by the price, including any unreviewed trade tasks, concealed-condition repairs, disposal, or finish work. Do not assume an exclusion or extra charge applies: agree on the actual list before authorizing the work.

Can I supply the materials for a priced project?

Discuss the products, quantities, and delivery arrangements before the price is agreed. The customer pays for materials and consumable supplies, but responsibility for selecting and obtaining them must also be confirmed.

What happens if the condition differs from the photos?

The scope may need further review. Confirm the condition, proposed change, revised price, and any schedule effect before extra work starts. Do not treat an initial photo-based discussion as approval of every concealed repair.

What payment schedule applies to a project?

Confirm the payment schedule and any deposit in the written agreement. This page does not establish a universal deposit policy. Review current Ontario consumer guidance when comparing the proposed terms.

Does a project price guarantee a lower total?

No. A negotiated project price describes the cost of an agreed scope, not an automatic discount. Compare the same tasks, finish, material responsibility, and exclusions when evaluating alternatives.

What records should I keep for a priced project?

Keep the agreed scope, estimate, payment terms, approved changes, invoices, and material receipts. These records help you compare the completed work with the arrangement that was authorized.
