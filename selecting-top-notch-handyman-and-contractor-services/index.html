Handyman or General Contractor in Toronto: How to Choose

Choose based on the work that needs to be done—not a blanket price threshold or a fixed rule about project size. A focused repair list may suit handyman work; a renovation that needs several trades or coordinated decisions may call for a general contractor. In either case, define the scope and check any permit or qualification requirements before booking.

Start by listing the tasks, the outcome you expect and any work that may need coordination. For a focused repair or maintenance list, compare it with handyman services in Toronto. For a renovation involving several phases or trades, review general contracting in Toronto. Ask who is responsible for coordination, permits and any specialist work that applies to your project.

Key Takeaways

  • Choose by scope, trade coordination and project responsibilities—not a universal dollar cutoff.
  • Check permit and qualification requirements for the specific work and location.
  • Agree on tasks, exclusions, materials, pricing and change approvals in writing.
  • Use the confirmed handyman rates as a starting point for labour, then confirm the actual scope.

When Handyman Work May Fit

Handyman service may be a fit when the task list is clear and the work is within the provider's scope. Share the full list, photos and material details in advance so the visit and applicable rate can be confirmed.

  • A defined list of repair or maintenance tasks that can be reviewed together.
  • Work where the labour schedule and material responsibility can be confirmed beforehand.
  • Tasks that do not require a different qualified trade or project coordination beyond the handyman scope.

When to Consider a General Contractor

Consider general contracting when the project needs a broader plan, coordinated phases or communication among several trades. Ask what the contractor will manage directly, what will be subcontracted and who will confirm required permits and inspections.

  • The project involves multiple phases that need sequencing.
  • Several trades or specialist providers may need to coordinate.
  • Permit, inspection or design responsibilities need to be clarified.
  • You want one agreed project scope with a clear process for decisions and changes.

Questions to Confirm Before Booking

  1. What exact tasks and finish are included, and what is excluded?
  2. Which pricing method and minimum apply to this scope?
  3. Who supplies materials, and how will their cost be shown?
  4. Does the work require a permit, inspection or qualified trade? Who will confirm and coordinate that?
  5. How will changes or additional tasks be priced and approved?

Project Coordination Questions

Who should coordinate work that depends on several trades?

Agree on a responsible coordinator before booking. Identify which tasks must happen first, who schedules the providers, and who checks that each stage is ready for the next. A repair list alone does not establish those responsibilities.

Does hiring a general contractor authorize electrical work?

No. For paid residential electrical work in Ontario, confirm the responsible Licensed Electrical Contractor and ECRA/ESA licence. A general contractor or individual electrician is not a substitute for the contracting business required by ESA.

Who should confirm permits and inspections?

Put the responsibilities in writing and check the exact work with the relevant municipality or regulator. An estimate, application submission, or payment is not evidence that an approval has been issued.

What if a repair reveals a larger problem?

Pause to review the condition and proposed next step. A larger diagnosis, trade repair, or renovation must be scoped and priced before additional work is authorized; the original task list should not silently expand.

Who is responsible for materials arriving on time?

Confirm who selects, purchases, and delivers each product. The customer pays for handyman materials and consumable supplies, but that price policy does not by itself assign ordering or delivery responsibilities.

What should I tell a contractor about a managed property?

Identify who may authorize the work, who provides access, permitted work hours, and any property-management requirements. Confirm those arrangements before committing to a schedule.

Can painting start immediately after a wall repair?

Confirm preparation, drying, and finish requirements with the responsible providers. Scheduling must follow the actual repair and product conditions rather than an assumed same-day sequence.

How should changes to a renovation scope be approved?

Record the added or changed tasks, price, material responsibility, and schedule effect before work proceeds. Keep the approved change with the original agreement.

What completion records should I request?

Compare the work with the agreed scope and retain invoices, approved changes, applicable inspection documents, and any written workmanship terms. Confirm which documents apply to your particular project.

Which service should I contact when the scope is unclear?

Send the full task list and describe the result you want. Ask whether the request fits handyman work, needs a qualified trade, or requires broader general-contractor coordination before selecting a booking path.
